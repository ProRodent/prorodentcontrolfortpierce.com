Why trouble often starts in the garage.

Few rooms hand rodents so much at once: shade, shelter, stored cardboard and cloth, and a bottom seal that wears out. Warm, damp weather makes the room comfortable, and a downpour can push animals toward it from soaked ground.

Many people only open the garage to park or grab a tool, so trouble can build for weeks unseen. Pellets on a shelf, shredded fabric in a tote or a chewed bag of seed are the usual first signs.

What gets inspected.

  • The overhead door's bottom strip, the side seals and each corner where the door touches the wall.
  • The wall-to-slab joint, where gaps tend to open.
  • Wall penetrations for pipes, wires and water or gas lines.
  • Shelves, totes, boxes and any spare freezer or appliance.
  • The attic hatch or ceiling access over the garage.

A garage is also a frequent link to the attic. Rodents climb inside wall cavities, and a gap at the top plate can lead from the garage straight to the roof space.

Once the animals are gone, exclusion work closes each opening. The trapping page describes how devices are placed so pets cannot reach them.

Seasonal gear and boat storage.

Holiday decorations, fishing tackle, boat covers and cushions that sit for months are perfect bedding. Anything stored for a season belongs in a lidded plastic bin, and it is worth lifting the lid whenever you are in the garage.

If your lot has a shed or other outbuilding, mention it on the call. Plenty of problems begin there and move toward the house.

Before the technician arrives.

Raise the door and let the room air for half an hour before stepping in, put on gloves and leave droppings unswept. Photograph what you find, and note any chewed cord so nobody plugs in a damaged appliance.

If a vehicle in the garage may hold a nest, the vehicle page lists what to check.

Getting the property ready.

  • Move things so the garage wall, the under-sink cabinet and the attic access are reachable.
  • Resist shaking out chewed cardboard, since that launches dust.
  • Place any snap trap well away from toddlers and big dogs.
  • Decide which room smells strongest and find words to describe the odor.

Careful cleanup in five small moves

Come back to this routine each time pellets, urine stains or shredded nesting turn up indoors.

  • A shop vacuum can seem thorough and still blow tiny particles back into the room.
  • Leave the doors of a closed-up area wide open for half an hour before you start.
  • You cannot see germs, so glove up first and wash last.
  • Disinfect first and collect afterward, after the soaked material has rested about five minutes.
  • Knot the bag and set it with ordinary household trash, and never lift a carcass with bare fingers.
  • We make no medical claims. Fever, aching all over or labored breathing after handling waste is a matter for a doctor.

Where the safety steps come from

Published guidelines back each safety point on this page, and none of them come from a company selling services.

  • CDC steps for preventing hantavirus exposure: Close the entries before dealing with old droppings and nests, and handle those with care.
  • CDC advice on cleaning rodent droppings and nests: A household damp-cleaning routine that explains the reason for each step.

Details worth having ready.

  • Gap under the garage door.
  • Items leaning on the walls or sitting in totes.
  • Items stored for months, like seasonal gear or boat supplies.
  • Any storage room, utility closet or basement involved.

Garage questions asked in Fort Pierce.

What makes a garage so appealing?

It has shade, shelter, stored materials and a door that seldom closes tight. When the weather turns warm and wet, a garage can feel like a safe nursery.

Do Fort Pierce houses have basements?

Basements are rare along much of the Florida coast, so a utility room, storage closet or shed often serves the same purpose. Tell us which space you have.

Are plastic bins better than cardboard?

Lidded plastic resists chewing and leaves less bedding for animals. Setting the bins on a shelf off the floor adds more protection.

Can I swap the door seal myself?

A new seal helps, but the corners and the threshold still need a look. The technician can tell you what to buy or install it.

Will you look at a detached shed?

Yes. Describe it on the call, including its floor and what is kept inside.
