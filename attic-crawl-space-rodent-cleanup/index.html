What the animals leave.

An infestation leaves more than the animals. Pellets, urine, nesting and shredded insulation stay in the attic or under-floor space for months, and warm, damp conditions make odors build. Cleanup takes out that waste and readies the space for new insulation.

The work waits until the entries are sealed. Otherwise new animals soil the cleaned area again and the effort is wasted. The exclusion page lays out the order of work.

Safe cleanup in steps.

The CDC's cleanup page describes a damp method. Let a shut-up space air out first, put on gloves, spray droppings until they are wet through and wipe them up with paper towels. Sweeping or vacuuming them dry stirs dust into the air.

  • Open hatches and doors and wait before going in.
  • Wear gloves and keep bare skin off soiled material.
  • Soak waste and the surface under it, then wait about five minutes.
  • Seal everything in bags and wash your hands afterward.

Attics in a hot climate can be stuffy and tight. If the space is big, heavily soiled or hard to reach, a professional crew with proper protective gear is the safer choice.

Fort Pierce buildings and our questions.

Buildings in this part of Florida range from slab-on-grade with an attic to raised floors over a crawl space, and we do not guess which one you have. Describe the way in, the ceiling height and any damp-looking patch, since moisture changes the plan.

With a raised floor, vents and skirting on the damp side deserve a close look. After heavy rain, check that screens are intact and no soil has washed against the foundation.

Once it is clean.

When the space is clean and dry, we can suggest new insulation and any damage repair. If you still hear activity, go back to trapping before more work is done.

Steps for the meantime.

  • A truck with debris under the hood stays parked until someone inspects it.
  • Carry grass seed, kibble and bird food indoors for now.
  • Drop any gnawed object in a bag and hold it for the technician.
  • Learn where the breaker panel is, in case a cable was chewed.

Safe handling of rodent waste and nest debris

Read each line before you touch anything, because a short pause protects your airways.

  • Hurrying lifts dust. Slow down and put the brush aside.
  • Open up, step away and wait half an hour, since hazards can linger in stagnant air.
  • Wear gloves for the entire task and wash up the moment it is over.
  • The sequence is spray, pause for five minutes, wipe.
  • One securely tied bag can hold pellets, towels and a carcass.
  • Notice how you feel in the weeks that follow. A fever or shortness of breath is a reason to see a doctor and describe what you handled.

Cleanup questions asked in Fort Pierce.

Why does cleanup come last?

Animals with access would soil the space again. Removal and sealing come first, then cleanup.

May I clean droppings myself?

Follow the CDC steps: air the space, wear gloves, soak the waste and never sweep or vacuum it dry. For a large attic, many people choose a professional.

Does humidity complicate the work?

Damp air slows drying and spreads odor, so treatment and ventilation get extra care in this climate.

Does every home here have a crawl space?

No. Buildings vary, from slab-on-grade with only an attic to raised floors over a crawl space, and the visit covers whatever yours has.

Can cleanup be booked fast?

Call at any hour. The schedule varies by day, and the dispatcher offers a window.
