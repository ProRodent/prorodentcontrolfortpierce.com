If your lot touches water.

Rodents prefer edges to open ground, which feels exposed. A canal bank, a pond edge or a strip of thick vegetation can serve as a quiet route toward a building. What lies past your property line is out of our hands, yet your side can be sealed and made less attractive.

Dense plantings and tropical landscaping.

Palms, hedges and lush groundcover look good and also hide movement. A palm crown full of old fronds can shelter roof rats, and a thick shrub against the wall can hide a gap in the siding. None of this is a reason to tear out plants. It is a reason to trim them back from the building and keep dead material cleared.

  • Keep shrubs a foot or two away from the walls.
  • Remove dead fronds and trim back from the roof.
  • Pick up fallen fruit, nuts and seed.
  • Keep pet food and bird seed in lidded tubs.

Humidity, storms and wear.

Constant damp air is hard on building parts. Wood trim swells, sealant cracks and metal screens corrode, any of which can open a gap. After a stormy stretch, check vents, door seals and the area where the roof meets the wall. If you find damage, the exclusion page explains how we close it.

Storage areas such as sheds, boat storage and outbuildings can hold damp cushions, covers and cardboard that make perfect nesting material. Use lidded plastic bins and check them when you open the space.

Vehicles and boats parked for a while.

If a boat trailer, golf cart or car sits unused, a nest can form in the engine bay or under a cover. Look before you start it, and see the vehicle page for what to check.

What to mention when you call.

Give your street, whether water is behind the lot, whether palms or trees touch the roof and where you noticed activity. Mention sheds, docks or stored boats. With those details the technician arrives knowing where to look first.

First moves at home.

  • Shut the door of the room showing the most signs.
  • Dry pellets crumble into breathable powder, so leave them where they lie.
  • Poisoned animals tend to crawl into a wall cavity or duct and die there.
  • Jot down the minute the scratching starts, since timing helps narrow the species.

Before you call: working around droppings without stirring dust

Read each line before you touch anything, because a short pause protects your airways.

  • Brushing dry droppings flings fine particles toward your face, so the broom stays put.
  • Open every door and window that will open, then wait thirty minutes before starting.
  • Wear gloves for the entire task and wash up the moment it is over.
  • Wet it all thoroughly, wait roughly five minutes, then pick the waste up with towels meant for the trash.
  • Everything you gather goes into a closed bag before it reaches the bin.
  • We make no medical claims. Fever, aching all over or labored breathing after handling waste is a matter for a doctor.
