Roof rat or Norway rat.

The two species leave different signs. Roof rats are lean climbers that nest high, in attics, soffits and the crowns of crowded palms. Norway rats are stockier and dig at ground level, beside slabs, sheds and foundations.

The distinction changes the job. A roof rat plan leans on rooflines, wires and tree limbs. A Norway rat plan leans on soil, drains and the base of walls. Naming the species first tells the technician which route to follow.

Reading the yard before the attic.

A rat repeats the same trail night after night, and a trail leaves marks. Watch for greasy smears along a fence rail or pipe, pellets on a shed roof and gnawed fruit or seed beneath a tree or feeder.

  • Walk the fence and note gaps under boards, burrows at posts and dark smudges on rails.
  • Find where palms, limbs and wires pass within a jump of the roof.
  • Check shed floors, deck edges and the soil beside the slab for holes about two inches across.
  • Look at soffits and roof vents for torn screens or chewed edges.

Taking the animals out.

Rats distrust new objects, so traps may sit unset for a day or two while the animals get used to them. Where a trap goes matters more than how many go out. Devices sit on the proven route, high for roof rats and along walls for Norway rats.

Each device is written in a log and checked on a timetable you are told about. That record, not a quiet attic, is how the crew knows the last rat is gone.

Shutting the door behind them.

Rats chew through foam, wood and thin plastic, so closing work uses metal flashing and heavy mesh held with hard sealant. Roof rats often enter high, where a frond or limb touches the eave, and cutting that bridge back is part of the visit.

While you wait for the visit.

Keep children and pets out of the attic and garage, leave droppings alone and carry pet food inside at night. Photograph any burrow or bitten fruit with a coin beside it to show size.

If rats have fouled the insulation, plan on attic and crawl space cleanup once the animals are gone. When you are unsure what you have, an inspection comes first.

While you wait.

  • A truck with debris under the hood stays parked until someone inspects it.
  • Carry grass seed, kibble and bird food indoors for now.
  • Drop any gnawed object in a bag and hold it for the technician.
  • Learn where the breaker panel is, in case a cable was chewed.

Careful cleanup in five small moves

These lines restate public health guidance, and any household can use them whether or not a visit ever gets booked.

  • Brushing dry droppings flings fine particles toward your face, so the broom stays put.
  • Open every door and window that will open, then wait thirty minutes before starting.
  • You cannot see germs, so glove up first and wash last.
  • Spray every pellet and stain with disinfectant, then let it stand for around five minutes.
  • Pick a bag that seals well, with gloves or a tool between you and the animal.
  • We make no medical claims. Fever, aching all over or labored breathing after handling waste is a matter for a doctor.

Why rat jobs start in the yard.

  • The path is outside.

    Rats usually arrive over a fence, along a wire, up a palm trunk or out of a burrow, so the visit opens in the yard and works up to the attic.

  • Bigger gaps, harder teeth.

    A rat can pass through roughly a half inch opening and chews soft fill, so the sealing material has to be metal and wire mesh.

  • Food sources named.

    Feeders, windfall fruit, pet dishes and compost each earn their own recommendation.

Rat questions asked in Fort Pierce.

How can I tell a rat from a mouse?

Judge by size. Rat pellets are close to a grain of rice or larger, and mouse pellets are smaller and pointed at the ends. Digging beside a slab and fruit with deep bites also suggest rats.

Do rats really nest in attics here?

They do. Roof rats climb well, and branches, fences, wires and palm trunks give them a ramp to the roof. Heavy footsteps around dusk are a common report.

Can I block the hole myself?

Please wait. A plug pushed in while a rat is inside may drive it deeper into the building, and a quick patch is often chewed straight back out.

Do palms bring rats?

Thick fronds can shelter them and give a path to the roof, and dropped fruit can feed them. Trimming and tidying reduce the pull, but sealing and trapping do the heavy lifting.

Is rat help available at night?

Yes. Someone picks up all night, and the schedule sometimes allows a visit that very day.
