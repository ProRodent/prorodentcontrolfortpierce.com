Questions we ask about a Port St. Lucie property.

We have no verified portrait of the city's streets, so we describe nothing about its housing and ask instead. Is it a detached house, a townhouse or a condo? Does a wall or garage touch a neighbor's? Are there palms, a fence or a canal edge behind the lot? The answers decide where the technician begins.

A large city holds every type of property, and each one has its own weak points. Shared walls carry animals between units, a detached house offers more edges to check and a rental needs the manager informed. We tailor the first visit to which one yours is.

Signs that justify a call.

  • Scratching or thumping in the ceiling soon after dark.
  • Dark pellets in a pantry, a garage shelf or beside the water heater.
  • Chewed boxes, cords or trim, or torn insulation.
  • A sharp urine smell, or a rotten smell with no source in sight.
  • A rodent seen indoors in daylight.

Any one of these deserves a phone call, and two at once usually mean animals are active now. Photograph what you find and note the date.

From walk-through to follow-up.

Work proceeds in sequence. First an inspection identifies the species and charts the routes. Next trapping and removal clears whatever is inside, and entry point sealing shuts the gaps with metal and mesh so more cannot arrive. A return visit confirms that the traps stayed quiet and the seals held.

If the walk-through turns up soiled insulation or gnawed material, cleanup of the attic or crawl space or damage repair could follow. Each stage has its own price line.

Heat, rain and the building.

Warm, damp weather lasts most of the year on this coast, and rodents stay active with it. A hard rain can send burrowing animals toward dry cover, and an air-conditioned building stays attractive in the hottest months. After a storm, check vent covers and the base of the walls for new holes.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep children and pets out of that room until a technician has looked at it.
  • Pull on disposable gloves before touching anything stained.
  • Sticky boards snare tree frogs, anoles and small birds as well as rodents.
  • Take photos first, because wiping away the evidence costs us clues.

The CDC cleanup method in everyday words

Residents can use the list to keep dust low while a technician is on the way.

  • Brooms, leaf blowers and household vacuums all raise the dust you are trying to avoid, so keep them away from dry waste.
  • Give a shed, garage or crawl space that has been shut a half hour of cross-breeze before going in.
  • Bare skin should never touch a contaminated spot.
  • Spray every pellet and stain with disinfectant, then let it stand for around five minutes.
  • Drop wipes and debris into a single bag, knot it and toss it, hands covered near any body.
  • We make no medical claims. Fever, aching all over or labored breathing after handling waste is a matter for a doctor.
