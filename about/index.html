Where a booking begins.

Ring (772) 710-1009 and the dispatcher asks four plain questions: the street, the sign you noticed, when it started and whether pets or children share the space. Those answers shape who goes out and what they bring.

The line rings through nights, weekends and holidays. If no technician can reach you the same day, you hear that during the call, along with a realistic window.

The outline of a visit.

  • Walk the roofline, attic, garage, crawl space and yard for fresh evidence.
  • Place traps or stations on whatever routes the evidence marks.
  • Cover openings with sheet metal, steel mesh and hard sealant.
  • Come back to count catches, test the repairs and plan any cleanup.

The sequence protects the work. Closing holes too early can entomb an animal in a wall, and scrubbing an attic while the doorways are open means scrubbing it again.

What this site leaves out.

No page here shows a founding year, a license number, a crew photo, a trophy or a customer quote. None reached us in a form we could verify, and an invented detail does more harm than a blank. When a technician arrives at your door, you are welcome to ask for credentials.

Nor can we promise that rodents will stay away for good. Palms, fences, drainage edges and the neighboring lot all keep animals nearby. Careful searching, durable materials and a second visit are what we can honestly offer.

Coverage and the footer address.

Service follows ZIP codes. Fort Pierce has seven in our data, and the areas page adds covered ZIP codes in Port St. Lucie and Vero Beach. Port St. Lucie is much larger than the part we reach, so its page lists only the codes that qualify.

The footer shows 7459 US Highway 1, Fort Pierce, FL 34982, which is a citation listing and not a place to drop by. That ZIP code is not one of our coverage codes. Each job starts on the phone and takes place at your property.

Poison, and your health.

The EPA limits how rodenticides are used, and its page is linked below. We prefer trapping plus sealing. If a bait product is ever used, you are told which one and where it sits.

For waste cleanup we follow the CDC sequence of airing out, wetting, wiping and bagging. Medical questions belong with your own physician, since pest work is the only field we can speak to.
