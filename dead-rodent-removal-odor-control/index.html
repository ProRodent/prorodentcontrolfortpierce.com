Why it smells and where it hides.

A dead rodent decays within days, and warm weather speeds it up. The smell can seep through a ceiling, move along a duct or rise from beneath a floor. Since the animal is usually hidden, the odor can seem to come from everywhere.

In a humid coastal climate, a hidden carcass can smell for a long time, and moisture helps spread it. The strongest room is a clue, not an answer.

Tracing the source.

  • Learn which room is worst and when the smell first appeared.
  • Check the attic, ceiling voids, crawl space, wall cavities and ducts nearest that room.
  • Follow flies, stains or fluid marks where present.
  • Open access panels or small inspection holes if needed.

Sometimes the animal is easy to find. Sometimes it takes patient work, and the plan is explained before any wall is opened.

Removing it safely.

Wear gloves, keep bare skin away from the animal and seal it in a bag. Soak the area with disinfectant and wipe with disposable towels, as the CDC cleanup page describes. Dry debris is never swept or vacuumed.

Stopping a repeat.

A dead rodent often means there were live ones first, so look for how it got in. Sealing, covered on the exclusion page, keeps new animals out. Trapping, described on the trapping page, clears any others.

If odor soaked into insulation or wood, attic and crawl space cleanup can remove the contaminated material. Where walls or ceilings were opened, damage repair puts them back.

Meanwhile.

Open windows when the weather allows, keep pets out of the affected room and run a fan if it helps move air outside. Skip strong chemical sprays in a closed room, and do not cut into walls yourself.

Note where the smell peaks and what time of day it is worst. Afternoon heat can make it stronger.

Before the technician arrives.

  • A truck with debris under the hood stays parked until someone inspects it.
  • Carry grass seed, kibble and bird food indoors for now.
  • Drop any gnawed object in a bag and hold it for the technician.
  • Learn where the breaker panel is, in case a cable was chewed.

While you wait for a technician: do this, skip that

Read each line before you touch anything, because a short pause protects your airways.

  • Mist anything dry before a hand, glove or towel comes near it.
  • Leave the doors of a closed-up area wide open for half an hour before you start.
  • Keep your hands covered the whole time, then lather well.
  • Keep spraying until no dry patch remains, then step away for about five minutes before wiping.
  • Pellets and wipes can share one tight bag, and a dead animal never touches bare skin.
  • We make no medical claims. Fever, aching all over or labored breathing after handling waste is a matter for a doctor.

Odor questions asked in Fort Pierce.

How long will the smell linger?

Odors often fade over a few weeks, though warm, damp weather can stretch that. Removing the source is the quickest cure.

Can an air freshener cover it up?

It hides the odor without removing the cause. The source has to go.

Why are flies showing up?

Flies are drawn to decay and can signal a dead animal nearby. Mention them when you call.

May I remove the animal myself?

If you can see it, wear gloves, use a sealed bag and wash your hands, following the CDC steps. If it is inside a wall or ceiling, call us.

Does the air conditioner spread the smell?

It can push odor along ducts, so say whether the smell changes when the system runs.
