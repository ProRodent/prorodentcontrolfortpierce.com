What we ask before assuming.

We hold no verified description of Vero Beach streets or housing, so we do not offer one. We ask about your own property. Is it a house, a condo or a shop? Is the roof tile, shingle or metal? Do palms or hedges touch the walls? Is there a garage, a shed or a boat or cart in storage?

Those details guide the technician. A roof rat job leans on the roofline and trees, a mouse job leans on pipes, doors and the slab edge, and a stored vehicle needs its own check.

Warning signs worth a phone call.

  • Noises in the attic or walls after dark.
  • Pellets in drawers, closets or garage totes.
  • Gnawed packaging, seed bags or cable sheathing.
  • Nest scraps in a vent cover or engine bay.
  • A smell that grows stronger on warm afternoons.

If you notice two or more, rodents are probably active now. A photograph with a coin for scale helps us judge the species from the phone.

How a visit unfolds.

The technician begins outdoors, looking over fences, slab edges, vents and the places where limbs touch the roof, and then heads for the attic and garage. Mouse control and rat control explain how each species is trapped, and afterward sealing the entry points shuts the gaps.

If a vehicle or boat trailer is involved, the vehicle page explains what we check. A shed or detached garage is covered under garage and storage work.

Seasonal gear and storm weather.

Seasonal gear, boat covers and stored cushions are ideal nesting material when they sit untouched. Use lidded plastic bins, and check them whenever you open the space. After heavy rain or a strong wind, look at vent screens and the roof edge for new gaps.

Small jobs for the wait.
