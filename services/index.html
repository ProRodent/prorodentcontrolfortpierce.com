Rodent trouble is rarely a single chore. Locating the animals, taking them out, closing their doorways and scrubbing up afterward call for separate tools. Below are twelve pages, one for each kind of question that reaches us.

With only a noise to go on, an inspection is the place to start. After the species is known, the mouse and rat pages dig further. Garages, vehicles and yards have their own pages, since the risks change from place to place.

How the services stack up.

A whole job has five stages in order, namely look, trap, seal, clean and repair. Cleanup waits near the end, since scrubbing an attic that still has tenants only creates repeat work.

Plenty of people need a single stage. An owner who already plugged every hole may want cleanup alone, and a restaurant manager may want regular checks. Tell us what is finished, and the plan covers what remains.

Urgent calls are not a separate tier, because the work itself does not change. A person answers all night, and any page here lets you ask for a same-day slot if one is open.

Where the safety steps come from

Complete instructions and the product restrictions are in the public guidelines listed below.

  • CDC steps for preventing hantavirus exposure: Staying healthy around rodents, with sealing, trapping and careful cleaning in that order.
  • CDC advice on cleaning rodent droppings and nests: The CDC's walk through gloves, ventilation, wet wiping and disposal for pellets and nest material.

Service questions.

Where do I begin?

If you cannot tell what is living in the building, open the inspection page. That visit identifies the animal, tracks down the entries and gives you a plan before any larger commitment.

May I order only sealing, or only cleanup?

You may. Tell us what is already finished, and the visit is planned around the rest.

Do cars, boats and yards count?

One page covers vehicles, from engine compartments to cabin air intakes, and another covers yards, with burrows, sheds, shrubs and feeders. Say so if your vehicle is a boat trailer or a cart.

Which page fits a shop or office?

The commercial page is for owners and managers. It explains scheduled checks, treatment and the report you get after each stop.
