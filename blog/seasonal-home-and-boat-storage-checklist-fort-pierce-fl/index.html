Closing Up a Seasonal House or Boat: Rodent Checklist

A building or boat that stands unused for weeks gives rodents time to explore. It lists what to handle before departure and what to inspect once you are back.

A seasonal home, rental or boat stands empty for stretches. That quiet is an invitation. This checklist helps you reduce the pull before you leave and check what happened when you return.

Before you leave.

  • Remove all food, including pet food and bird seed.
  • Empty and clean the pantry, and take out the trash.
  • Store linens, cushions and clothing in lidded plastic bins.
  • Close gaps you know about, and check door and garage seals.
  • Trim plants and fronds back from the walls and roof.
  • Set up a periodic check by a friend or manager.

A clean, closed building offers less than a cluttered one. Cardboard on the floor, open bags and fabric piles are the first things animals use.

On arrival, before you clean.

Open doors and windows and let the building air out for about thirty minutes. Wear gloves. Walk each room and look for droppings, gnawed items, nests and smells. Check the pantry, closets, under sinks and the garage.

Boats, trailers and carts.

Stored boats can hold nests in cushions, covers and compartments. Check them before use, and look at trailer wiring and any covers. For vehicles, see our guide on cars, carts and boat trailers.

Cleaning safely.

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings. Spray them wet with disinfectant and let it sit about five minutes. Wipe with paper towels, bag the waste and wash your hands. The CDC page linked below explains the steps.

Seal and recheck.

If you find signs, the entries must be located and closed. The exclusion page lists the materials, and an inspection will find the gaps. Trapping removes the animals first.

Rentals and shared properties.

If you manage a rental or share a property with other owners, communication matters as much as the checklist. Tell guests to keep food in sealed containers and to take out trash before they leave. Ask the cleaning crew to report droppings, gnawing or odors rather than simply cleaning over them, because early reports let you act before a small problem becomes a large one.

Keep a short written log for each property: the date of every check, who did it and what they found. If animals do appear, that record shows how long the problem has been developing, and it helps a technician decide where to start.

A pre-departure routine you can repeat.

  • Walk each room and check for open food, crumbs and loose cardboard.
  • Empty the trash and clean the kitchen thoroughly.
  • Close the garage door and check its seal.
  • Take photographs of closets, the pantry and the garage so changes are easy to spot.
  • Leave a note for whoever checks the property, listing what to look for.

When to call.

Phone any hour if you find droppings, nests or gnawing, or smell something unpleasant on arrival. Tell us how long the property stood empty.

While you wait for a technician: do this, skip that

These lines restate public health guidance, and any household can use them whether or not a visit ever gets booked.

  • Wet it first, wipe it second, and sweep nothing.
  • Hot, stuffy storerooms and attics should air for thirty minutes before cleaning begins.
  • Gloves go on at the start and your hands go under the tap at the end.
  • Spray every pellet and stain with disinfectant, then let it stand for around five minutes.
  • Everything you gather goes into a closed bag before it reaches the bin.
  • Feeling feverish or short of breath after exposure is a question for a physician, so describe the cleanup to one.

More answers.

Do empty homes attract rodents?

They can. Quiet, unused rooms give animals time to explore, and any leftover food may draw them.

What should I leave out?

No food and no cardboard on the floor. Store linens and cushions in sealed bins.

Is it safe to enter after weeks away?

Air the building out for about thirty minutes first, wear gloves and look for signs before cleaning.

Do boats get rodents?

Stored boats, covers and cushions can host nests, so check them before use.

Should I ask someone to check while I am away?

A periodic check can catch trouble early. Ask them to report droppings, gnawing and smells.
