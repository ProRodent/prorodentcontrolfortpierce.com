What draws rodents to a parked vehicle.

To a rodent, a parked vehicle looks like a shelter. The engine bay holds warmth after a drive, the air box and cabin filter give soft nesting spots, and the underside blocks the weather. Time makes it worse, since a longer stay means a more settled animal.

Florida's mild climate keeps animals active all year. A vehicle left at a seasonal home, in a storage lot or in a driveway under palms can be inviting in any month.

How the visit goes.

  • Examine the engine compartment, the intake box, the cabin filter and the underside.
  • Remove nests and droppings with gloves, using the CDC damp-cleaning approach.
  • Note any chewed wiring and suggest a mechanic if needed.
  • Suggest ways to make the parking spot less attractive.

We do not repair wiring, because that is a mechanic's job. We clear the animals and the nest and tell you exactly what we saw, so the mechanic knows where to start.

Handling it safely.

Put on gloves before cleaning and avoid stirring up dust. Wet droppings with disinfectant rather than blowing or vacuuming them. The CDC cleanup page linked below lays out the steps.

A vehicle shut in a closed garage for a long time can also hold stale air. Open the doors and let it breathe before you climb in.

Keeping it from happening again.

Park away from palms, woodpiles and stacked materials when you can, and keep food and trash out of the cabin. If the vehicle sits for weeks, look under the hood and into the cabin vents before starting it. A sealed garage, if you have one, is a safer place to park.

When the garage is where it started, the garage page covers that room and the exclusion page explains the sealing. A yard visit lowers the pressure outside.

Small jobs before we come.

  • Stay out of the attic and the space under the floor, since moving insulation throws grit into the air.
  • Leave a nest alone, because a startled mother may retreat deeper into the structure.
  • Work out where the attic hatch and the crawl door are before anyone arrives.
  • If a golf cart or truck shows nest debris, park it outdoors until it is checked.

Safe handling of rodent waste and nest debris

Thinking of tidying before the visit? Take the steps in order, and pause at any that seems unclear.

  • Anything dry stays off the brush and out of the vacuum.
  • Give a shed, garage or crawl space that has been shut a half hour of cross-breeze before going in.
  • You cannot see germs, so glove up first and wash last.
  • The sequence is spray, pause for five minutes, wipe.
  • Used towels and any dead rodent go into a plastic bag, and bare skin stays off the body.
  • If fever, body aches or trouble breathing appear within weeks, see a doctor and mention the exposure. We handle pests, and medicine belongs to physicians.

Vehicle questions asked in Fort Pierce.

Why would a rodent nest in a vehicle?

An engine bay gives shelter, leftover warmth and soft material. A vehicle parked for days gives the animal time to settle in.

What are the warning signs?

Pellets under the hood, shredded filler in the air box, a musty odor when the fan runs, or a dashboard light that came on after parking.

Is it safe to start the engine?

If chewed wiring is possible, have it checked first. Running an engine with damaged wiring can create a hazard.

Do boat trailers and golf carts count?

Yes. Anything stored for a while with warm engine parts or soft covers can host a nest.

Will I need a mechanic?

If wiring is chewed, a mechanic repairs it. We remove the animal and the nest, and we tell you what to point out.
