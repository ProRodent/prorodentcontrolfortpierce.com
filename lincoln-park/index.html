What we can say about Lincoln Park, and what we cannot.

Our research notes list Lincoln Park as a historic area of Fort Pierce, but we could not confirm any historic designation, a surveyed boundary, a street list or a building date. So this page does not call it a historic district and does not describe its houses. For a booking, what counts is the street address you give on the phone.

Whatever the history, the useful questions are practical. How old is your roof? Are there gaps at the vents or soffits? Do trees touch the building? Those answers drive the plan.

Older and newer materials.

We cannot say which buildings here are older or newer. If yours is older, the materials may have shrunk, split or pulled away over time, and any gap can let a mouse through. If yours is newer, builders sometimes leave openings around pipes and vents that no one sees until an animal uses them. Age by itself is not the problem. Gaps are.

Trees, limbs and the roofline.

A mature tree gives shade and also a ladder. A limb that touches the roof lets a roof rat step from branch to shingle. We recommend trimming limbs back several feet from the building and removing dead branches. If you cannot trim safely, a tree service can help.

  • Trim limbs away from the roof and siding.
  • Keep shrubs back from the walls.
  • Remove dead fronds, vines and debris from the roofline.
  • Keep pet food and fallen fruit picked up.

Humidity, storms and old gaps.

Warm, damp coastal weather stays long, and rodents stay active with it. Heavy rain can send animals toward a building for dry cover. After a storm, check vents, seals and the base of the walls. Small gaps that were ignored in spring can turn into doorways later.

Close lots and shared fences.

When yards are close, an animal can move between them in a night. Tell neighbors if you find a burrow or droppings, and ask them to check their own side. Sealing your building helps, and shared effort keeps the results lasting. The yard page covers burrows and food sources.

What to mention when you call.

Open with the street or nearest cross street, add the rough age of the roof if you know it and name any trees touching the building. Describe what you noticed and when it began. With that, the technician starts in the right place.

Steps for the meantime.

  • Pellets, nests and gnawed cords are off limits to small children and pets.
  • Prop open a shut-up shed or garage and give it time to air before anyone goes in.
  • Traps you already set can stay exactly where they are.
  • Mention any storm cleanup, roof work, palm trimming or shifted storage from the past month.

The CDC cleanup method in everyday words

None of this needs a phone call. It echoes what health agencies tell every household that finds droppings.

  • Mist anything dry before a hand, glove or towel comes near it.
  • Leave the doors of a closed-up area wide open for half an hour before you start.
  • Keep your hands covered the whole time, then lather well.
  • Coat the waste and the patch beneath it, wait five minutes and blot with throwaway towels.
  • Everything you gather goes into a closed bag before it reaches the bin.
  • Unwell a few weeks later? Tell a clinician and mention the rodent contact.

Lincoln Park, answered.

Is Lincoln Park a historic district?

Our notes label it historic, but no designation could be confirmed, so we make no such claim. For a rodent job the roof, the vents and the gaps in the walls matter more than any label.

Do older buildings have more rodents?

Age alone does not decide it. Gaps matter more, a newer building can have them as well, and the inspection settles the question.

Can you work around mature trees?

Yes. We note which limbs touch the roof and tell you what to trim, and we seal the gaps the animals use.
